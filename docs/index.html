02 / FEATURED THESIS

Graduate research · in progress

Computational Prioritization of Cyclic Hexapeptides for JAK1, JAK2, and JAK3 Using Molecular Docking, Machine Learning, and Molecular Dynamics

Three related kinase targets.
One integrated computational workflow.

Background

JAK-family kinases are related therapeutic targets. Their similarity makes cross-target interpretation important when prioritizing cyclic-peptide candidates. Computational ranking can generate hypotheses, but cannot establish biochemical potency or cellular selectivity.

Objectives

Screen CX4C cyclic hexapeptides across JAK1/2/3, examine target-wise docking profiles and poses, evaluate sequence-based models trained on docking-derived labels, and follow selected complexes with MD.

Methods

Peptide generation and structural checks → receptor/ligand preparation → GPU docking and quality control → score-profile analysis → ML validation → 3D interaction assessment → GROMACS trajectories.

DYNAMIC FOLLOW-UP

Earlier 100 ns MD panel

CPYWWC, CGGWWC, and CWGGWC were examined against JAK1, JAK2, and JAK3 in three independent trajectories per complex. Peptide RMSD describes structural movement after receptor alignment, not binding affinity.

A separate five-peptide × three-JAK panel is ongoing. Its results are not shown as completed.

Discussion

Docking narrows a large candidate space; sequence models probe patterns in docking-derived labels; pose inspection assesses structural plausibility; MD adds dynamic context. These layers support prioritization, not a claim of experimental inhibition. Biochemical testing and orthogonal validation remain necessary.
